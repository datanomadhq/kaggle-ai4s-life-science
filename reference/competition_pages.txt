##### PAGE Description
The **AI for Life Science Challenge** invites you to explore how AI can advance life science, with a special focus on **organ-on-a-chip technologies and applications.**

Use AI, including machine learning, multimodal models, and AI agents, to explore organ-on-a-chip and other biological data. From analyzing cell images and predicting drug responses to supporting experiments and research workflows, we welcome new ideas that bring AI into life science.

**The only official submission is a Kaggle Writeup, which should include the demo video, public code repository, and technical report.** Additional links to interactive demos, online applications, Notebook Apps, web systems, or AI Agent systems can also be provided.

In order to helps reviewers understand the project's positioning and calibrate expectations accordingly, we recommdond the category below, The submission category does not affect the required deliverables — all teams submit the same Kaggle Writeup package. 


## Submission Categories

**Model & Algorithm**
Novel AI/ML models, algorithms, or computational methods for organ-on-a-chip applications, such as prediction models, image analysis algorithms, simulation methods, or scoring systems.

**Tool & Platform**
Software tools, analytical platforms, or infrastructure supporting organ-on-a-chip research workflows, such as data pipelines, experiment automation tools, or visualization platforms.

**End-to-End System**
Complete systems integrating multiple components from data input to actionable output, such as drug screening systems, automated experimental platforms, or AI-driven digital twins.

The official submission is a Kaggle Writeup, including the demo video, public code repository, and technical report. Optional links to interactive demos, online applications, Notebook Apps, web systems, or AI Agent systems may also be provided.



## Where will you make an impact?


**Drug Toxicity Prediction and Response Evaluation**

Develop AI models using organ-on-a-chip data, high-content imaging, or cell images to predict drug toxicity, efficacy, and dose-response relationships. Models may analyze imaging data and experimental metadata to identify cellular changes and generate risk scores or response indicators.

**Bright-field to Fluorescence Image Translation**

Explore in silico labeling methods to generate fluorescence images from bright-field images. The goal is to reduce staining costs and experimental burden while preserving biologically meaningful structures and enabling downstream analysis.

**Single-cell Phenotype Analysis**

Develop AI methods for cell segmentation, feature extraction, and phenotype analysis from organ-on-a-chip or microscopy images. Approaches may use supervised, unsupervised, or self-supervised learning to identify cell states, drug responses, and abnormal phenotypes.

**And Beyond:** Explore other innovative AI applications in organ-on-a-chip and life science.

##### PAGE Submission Requirements
Please read the following requirements carefully. Projects that do not meet these requirements will not be eligible for awards.

**Category Declaration: Each team must declare their submission category at the beginning of their Kaggle Writeup.**

### 1. Demo Video (Required)

Teams must include a public demo video showing the project workflow, core functions, main results, and practical value.

- Duration: Maximum 5 minutes. If longer, only the first 5 minutes are guaranteed to be reviewed.
- Format: Upload as a Kaggle attachment or provide a public link (e.g., YouTube, Bilibili, Loom, etc.).
- Access: The video must be viewable without login, permission requests, or payment.
- Content: The video should demonstrate the actual system or model operation, including inputs, workflow, outputs, key interfaces, or experimental results.
- Authorization: All materials, data, images, music, model outputs, or third-party content must have proper usage and redistribution rights.

### 2. Public Code Repository (Required)

Teams must provide a publicly accessible code repository link (e.g., GitHub, GitLab, Kaggle Notebook, or other platforms). The code must be reproducible by reviewers without requiring paid services, proprietary hardware, or non-public datasets.

The repository should allow reviewers to understand and reproduce the project, including:

- Core code;
- README.md;
- Environment setup files (e.g., requirements.txt, environment.yml, Dockerfile);
- Model files or download instructions;
- Testing, inference, evaluation, or demo scripts;
- Input/output descriptions;
- Reproduction instructions.

If external APIs, cloud services, commercial models, or third-party platforms are used, teams should describe required configurations, limitations, and alternative running options.

A clear entry script (e.g., demo.py, inference.py, evaluate.py) is recommended.

### 3. Technical Report (Required)

Teams must provide a technical report in the Kaggle Writeup. The report may be written directly in the Writeup or provided as a publicly accessible PDF link inserted in the Writeup. The report should be concise and self-contained, ideally within 15–20 pages (excluding references and appendices).

Recommended contents include:

- Project title, team information, and summary;
- Problem definition and application scenario;
- Data sources, licenses, processing methods, and compliance;
- Methods, models, algorithms, system architecture, or AI Agent workflow;
- Implementation details and technical components;
- Experiments, results, and evaluation;
- Reliability analysis and limitations;
- Potential scientific or practical impact;
- Reproduction instructions;
- Sources and licenses of external models, libraries, datasets, or AI tools.

### 4. Demo Application or Interactive System (Optional)

Projects with interactive demos, online applications, Notebook Apps, web systems, AI Agents, APIs, or simulation platforms may provide public access links.

- Links must be publicly accessible without login, approval, or payment.
- Usage instructions and technical requirements should be provided if needed.
- If the demo depends on temporary services or limited resources, teams should provide limitations and backup materials (e.g., screenshots, videos, or local execution instructions).

- **Code Repository Link**

- **Optional Demo Link**

##### PAGE Evaluation
**Show, don’t just tel**l. Your submission should clearly demonstrate the value of your AI solution through a compelling video demo and a detailed technical report. This is your opportunity to showcase not only what you built, but also why it matters, how it works, and what impact it can create in life science.

Your video should tell a clear story: introduce the problem, explain your approach, demonstrate your solution, and highlight the results. Your technical report should provide the details behind your work, including methodology, data sources, experimental design, results, and limitations.

Judges will evaluate submissions based on the following criteria:

**Problem Importance & Potential Impact (30%)**
Does the project address an important challenge in life science? Is the proposed solution meaningful and capable of creating practical or scientific value?

**Technical Approach & Innovation (30%)**
Is the AI method technically sound and innovative? Does the project effectively apply AI techniques to solve the proposed problem? Are the methodology and implementation clearly explained?

**Results & Validation (20%)**
Are the results reliable and well demonstrated? Does the project provide meaningful evaluation, comparisons, or experiments to support its effectiveness?

**Reproducibility & Implementation Quality (10%)**
Can the work be reproduced or further developed? Are the submitted code, models, datasets, and technical materials sufficiently documented and accessible for testing? Projects with limited reproducibility or low testability may receive lower scores in this category.

**Presentation Quality (10%)**
Does the video demo clearly communicate the problem, solution, and impact? Is the story engaging and easy to understand? Does the demonstration effectively showcase the value of the project?
